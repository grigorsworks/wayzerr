# If something goes wrong

## A. Security incident or data breach
1. **Contain:** rotate keys and tokens, take down the affected service, preserve logs. Don't delete evidence.
2. **Assess:** what data (emails? tokens?), how many people, since when.
3. **Notify:** if personal data is likely at risk, notify the data protection authority **within 72 hours** (EU/UK) and affected people without undue delay. US states have their own deadlines. Talk to your lawyer and insurer **immediately**; many policies require early notice.
4. **Fix and learn:** patch, ship an update, write a short public note, update the checklist.

## B. A legal threat, demand letter or lawsuit
1. **Don't reply, delete anything, or admit anything.** Don't argue by email or social media.
2. Save the message and all related logs, code and emails (a "legal hold").
3. Forward it to your insurer (claims notice often has strict deadlines) and your lawyer. Note any deadline in the letter.
4. Common drive-by claims and first checks:
   - *Accessibility (ADA/EAA):* which page/feature? Compare to `accessibility.html`, fix, keep a dated record.
   - *Cookies, Google Fonts, tracking (GDPR, CIPA/wiretap):* confirm the site loads no third-party resources (open the browser dev tools Network tab on the page and check that nothing loads from another domain). The home page sets no cookies and loads nothing external.
   - *Copyright or trademark:* identify the exact file/name, take it down if in doubt, then discuss.
   - *Provider terms (Anthropic, OpenAI, etc.):* pause the affected feature if told to; ask for specifics in writing.
   - *User harmed by a bot action:* gather their report, the app version and any logs they volunteer; do not promise compensation; refer to the Terms (sections 5, 13, 14) only through your lawyer.
5. A cheap first step is a short, polite lawyer's reply; many such letters stop there.

## C. Takedown or abuse report
Acknowledge within 2 working days, look into it, act, and keep a record.
